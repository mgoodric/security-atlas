Some records change what your organisation has committed to, so one
person should not be able to make that change alone. Four kinds of
record need an approval before they take effect: an exception to a
control, the acceptance of a risk, a policy under review and a framework
scope in review. This page collects everything waiting for you, so you
don't have to visit each area to find it.

## The second-person rule

The person who asks for an approval cannot also give it. Whoever
requested the exception, proposed the risk acceptance, or submitted the
policy or scope for review needs someone else to approve it. That keeps
every commitment checked by two people, which is what auditors expect to
see.

A small team may not have two people for every decision. An admin can
turn on **Allow self-approval** in the organisation settings. With it
on, your own requests appear here too, labelled as your request, and
approving one marks the record **Self-approved** wherever it appears,
including exports and the audit log. Only a person ever approves: an API
key or other machine credential never does, whatever the setting.

## What this page shows

Each waiting record, grouped by kind, oldest first, with who asked and
when. Open a record to read it and decide on its own page. An empty
page means nothing is waiting for you.

## Who can do what

- **Approve**: approvers, which means admins and GRC engineers. Each
  record also follows its own rules, which its page explains.
- **See this page**: everyone. If you are not an approver, it stays
  empty.
- **Turn on self-approval**: admins.
