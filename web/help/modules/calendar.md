A security program runs on dates: an audit starts, an exception runs
out, a policy is due for its yearly review, a vendor needs reassessing.
Missing one of those is how a program drifts. The compliance calendar
gathers every such date in one place, so nobody has to keep a separate
spreadsheet of deadlines.

## Where the dates come from

Each event is read from the record it belongs to, so the calendar is
never edited by hand. Change a policy's review date and the calendar
follows. The calendar shows:

- audit period milestones;
- exceptions about to expire;
- policy reviews, and vendor reviews on each vendor's review cadence;
- controls that need a periodic review;
- risk reviews and action plan due dates;
- policy acknowledgements and offboarding deadlines.

Overdue items are marked, so they stand out from what is merely coming
up.

## What this page shows

The agenda lists events by month. The month view puts them on a grid;
pick a day to see its events. Filter by event type, and share the
filtered view by its link. Open an event to go to its record.

**Subscribe in your calendar** gives you a personal, read-only link that
Google, Apple or Outlook Calendar can follow, so the dates appear next to
your meetings and update by themselves. Treat the link like a password:
anyone who has it can read your calendar's events.

## Who can do what

- **See the calendar** and **subscribe**: everyone who can sign in. You
  see the events of the areas your role can read.
- **Change a date**: on the record itself, under that record's rules.
